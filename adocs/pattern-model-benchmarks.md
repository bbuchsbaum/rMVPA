# pattern_model benchmarks (Phase 3)

Generated by `inst/benchmarks/pattern_model/bench_pattern_model.R` on 2026-09-07,
macOS, R 4.5, single core. Regenerate with

```
Rscript inst/benchmarks/pattern_model/bench_pattern_model.R
```

Timings cover the **whole workflow**: three outer folds, each running nested
rank selection (and penalty selection where the penalty is tuned), the refit,
and prediction. They are not single solver iterations.

| 100,000 | 400 | sparse = auto | 77.5 | 2,257 |
| 100,000 | 400 | sparse = 0.2, signed_smooth = 4 | 54.2 | 2,084 |

Cost is close to linear in the feature count. A whole-brain penalized analysis
with the penalty strength cross-validated runs in about 80 seconds at 100,000
features, on one core.

The data itself is 320 MB at p = 100,000, n = 400, against a 2.2 GB peak. The
factor of seven is the handful of full-size copies the fit needs (the screening
copy, the centred copy, the whitened copy in the initialization, and the
residual matrix for covariance estimation).

Earlier measurements on the same machine, before the step-size and outer-loop
fixes described in the plan, were 149 s at 30,000 and 540 s at 100,000 with
`sparse = auto`. Most of the difference is the corrected Lipschitz constant:
the step had been about `n` times too short, so the solver needed roughly ten
times as many inner iterations to travel the same distance.

## 2. Where regional accuracy is lost

Same assessment rows, same regions, four prediction heads. Rows 1 to 3 share
the whole-brain patterns `A_R` and differ **only** in the residual covariance,
so a gap among them is a covariance-model effect. Row 4 fits an independent
local model, so a gap between the best of rows 1 to 3 and row 4 is a missed
signal subspace rather than a covariance problem.

Signal-to-noise is deliberately low (0.08). At higher values every head scores
1.000 and the comparison says nothing.

| region | restricted (diag) | restricted (diag + low rank) | adapted (local shrinkage) | independent shrinkage LDA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.725 | 0.725 | 0.725 | 0.662 |
| 2 | 0.650 | 0.650 | 0.625 | 0.625 |
| 3 (local nuisance only) | 0.263 | 0.225 | 0.263 | 0.300 |
| 4 (redundant with 1) | 0.688 | 0.675 | 0.700 | 0.625 |

Reading:

- **The covariance model is not the bottleneck here.** Diagonal, diagonal plus
  low rank, and a locally re-estimated shrinkage covariance are within 0.025 of
  each other in every region. The concern recorded in the plan, that a few
  global noise components cannot represent local covariance, does not bite on
  this simulation.
- **The shared whole-brain subspace is not costing accuracy either.** In the
  three regions that carry signal, the restricted predictor matches or beats an
  independently fitted, tuned shrinkage LDA on the same rows, by 0.025 to 0.063.
  Borrowing the whole-brain task subspace helps more than fitting the region
  alone, at this sample size.
- **Region 3 is the honest negative control.** It carries only localized
  nuisance and no task signal, and every head sits at or below chance (0.333).
  Nothing recovers signal that is not there.

The caveat the plan states still stands: the restricted predictor inherits the
whole-brain task subspace, so it measures information accessible locally *under
that shared representation*, not the maximum a local method could extract.

## 3. Compiled-kernel decision

The plan gates a C implementation of the proximal step on whether the A-step
dominates at whole-brain scale.

| p | fit seconds | inner iterations | seconds per iteration | A-step share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100,000 | 4.9 | 21 | 0.008 | 3% |

**Decision: no compiled kernel.** At 100,000 features the entire penalized
A-step is about 3% of a fit. Each iteration costs 8 ms because it does no
`n x p` work: `X'Y_w` and the constant trace term are computed once per fit,
leaving `O(p r^2 + nnz(L) r)` per iteration. The remaining time is in the
initialization decompositions, the noise estimation, and the full-size copies,
none of which a proximal kernel would touch. A C implementation would add build
complexity for at most a few percent.

Revisit if a future penalty needs far more inner iterations, or if profiling
after the Phase 3b total-variation solver shows a different split.

## Confirmation inference validation (2026-09-07)

The reproducible script `inst/benchmarks/pattern_model/validate_confirmation.R`
adds 1,000 null experiments over 40 generated designs with 30 independent blocks.
At nominal 5%, the CR1 Wald approximation rejected 8.4% (mean p 0.4549), while
the restricted block wild bootstrap with 199 draws rejected 4.1% (mean p 0.4992).
The maximum-statistic omnibus family rejected in 3/40 designs. This small family
count does not establish a precise family-wise rate. CR1 remains available as an
explicit approximation; the worked confirmation guide uses the bootstrap.

A 400-row, 2,000-feature, rank-2 CR1 regression with 40 blocks took 0.032 seconds
and retained 242,584 bytes of fit summaries on R 4.5.1 / macOS arm64 / Accelerate
BLAS. The complete null simulation took 23.788 seconds. The implementation uses
one QR decomposition and rank-sized per-feature covariance, with vectorized
rank-1 and rank-2 statistics; no dense n-by-n or p-by-p covariance is allocated.


## Group sufficient-statistic validation (2026-09-07)

`inst/benchmarks/pattern_model/validate_group.R` generates known Gaussian subject
estimates independently of the confirmation regression. Across 2,000 rank-2
null experiments with 24 subjects, omnibus mean p was 0.5021 and the nominal 5%
rejection fraction was 5.0%. Estimated mean between-subject covariance trace was
0.2492 versus true 0.25. A heterogeneous-precision stress case gave mean p 0.5031
and rejection 4.6%; this supports the tested regime, not exact inference for
arbitrary heterogeneity.

The 24-subject / 2,000-feature random-effects workload took 7.723 seconds and
retained 9,224,552 bytes on R 4.5.1 / macOS arm64 / Accelerate BLAS. Group pooling
transports one feature's subject covariances at a time and retains aligned
coefficients, mean/between covariance, identity mappings, and provenance.
